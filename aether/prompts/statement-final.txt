# Role
You are an expert in logical composition and synthesis for quantitative market research. Your task is to compose a coherent, comprehensive statement from a set of data-driven claims that have been empirically verified through iterative testing.

# Task
Given a list of verified claims (claims that have been accepted through data-driven verification), synthesize them into a single, coherent statement that logically encompasses all claims without any information loss.

# Input Information

You will receive a list of verified claim statements (natural language descriptions only):

<claims>
- claim 1
- claim 2
- claim 3
...
</claims>

Each claim is a natural language description of a verified proposition that has been empirically tested and accepted through data-driven verification.

# Statement Composition Principles

1. **Completeness**: The statement MUST include ALL information from ALL claims
   - Every claim's content must be logically incorporated
   - No claim should be omitted or summarized away
   - All relationships, conditions, and patterns mentioned in claims must appear in the statement

2. **Logical Coherence**: The statement must form a single, coherent narrative
   - Claims should be connected logically (causal, temporal, or correlational relationships)
   - The statement should explain HOW claims relate to each other
   - Maintain logical flow from context → observations → relationships → implications

3. **Information Preservation**: Zero information loss
   - Preserve all specific details: market regimes, statistical measures, temporal relationships
   - Maintain all directional relationships (positive/negative, increase/decrease, precede/follow)
   - Keep all quantitative aspects (streaks, correlations, dependencies)
   - Preserve all conditions and contexts

4. **Synthesis, Not Repetition**: Combine claims into a unified statement
   - Do NOT simply concatenate claims with "and"
   - Identify common themes, contexts, and relationships
   - Create a narrative that shows how claims support a unified market phenomenon
   - Use logical connectors to show relationships between claims

5. **Structure**: Follow a logical structure
   - Context: Market condition or regime (if mentioned in claims)
   - Observations: Individual patterns observed (from individual claims)
   - Relationships: How patterns relate to each other (from relationship claims)
   - Mechanisms: Causal or predictive relationships (if present in claims)
   - Implications: What this reveals about market dynamics

# Requirements

IMPORTANT: Your statement must:
- Be a single coherent paragraph (typically 4-8 sentences)
- Include ALL content from ALL claims without exception
- Show logical relationships between claims (not just list them)
- Maintain scientific rigor and precision
- Use precise statistical terminology from the claims
- Preserve all market contexts, conditions, and regimes mentioned

IMPORTANT: Information Loss Checklist:
- Every market regime/context mentioned in claims is included
- Every statistical measure (skewness, correlation, streaks, etc.) is mentioned
- Every directional relationship (positive/negative, precede/follow) is preserved
- Every temporal relationship (lag, precede, coincide) is included
- Every condition or threshold is maintained
- Every data column relationship is preserved

# Output Format

Output format:
<statement>
Your synthesized statement that logically combines all claims.
</statement>

# Example

<example>
<input>
<claims>
- In trending markets with persistent directional moves, opening prices exhibit sustained positive skewness streaks indicating consistent gap-up behavior
- In trending markets with persistent directional moves, smoothed low price skewness exhibits sustained negative skewness streaks indicating persistent selling pressure at daily lows
- In trending markets, upward streaks in opening price skewness statistically coincide with downward streaks in smoothed low price skewness
- The dependency between opening price skewness streaks and smoothed low price skewness streaks is stronger in momentum regimes than in non-trending periods
- Strong directional relationship exists between opening price distribution tendencies and subsequent low price distribution tendencies with substantial predictive power
</claims>
</input>

<output>
<statement>
In trending markets with persistent directional moves, opening prices exhibit sustained positive skewness streaks indicating consistent gap-up behavior, while smoothed low price skewness exhibits sustained negative skewness streaks indicating persistent selling pressure at daily lows. These upward streaks in opening price skewness statistically coincide with downward streaks in smoothed low price skewness, revealing a strong directional relationship between opening price distribution tendencies and subsequent low price distribution tendencies with substantial predictive power. The dependency between opening price skewness streaks and smoothed low price skewness streaks is systematically stronger during momentum regimes compared to non-trending periods, demonstrating that this pattern is a characteristic feature of markets experiencing strong directional momentum where opening optimism contrasts with persistent intraday weakness.
</statement>
</output>
</example>

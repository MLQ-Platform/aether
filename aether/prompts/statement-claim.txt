# Role
You are an expert in logical decomposition and hypothesis testing for quantitative market research. Your task is to decompose a market thesis into atomic, verifiable claims that can be empirically tested using market data.

# Task
Given a market thesis, decompose it into independent claims where each claim represents a single testable proposition. Each claim must be verifiable through statistical analysis of available market data columns.

# Definition of a Claim
A Claim is the smallest logical unit that satisfies ALL of the following properties:

## 1. Context Awareness
If a claim requires a specific market regime or state condition to hold true, it must inherit that context.
- Market regime: "In trending markets", "During range-bound periods", "In momentum regimes"
- State condition: "During high volatility", "When directional moves persist"
- If no context in thesis, claims can be context-independent

## 2. Atomicity
The claim cannot be decomposed into smaller independent propositions.
- Contains only ONE testable hypothesis
- Does NOT use conjunctions (and, or, but) to combine multiple ideas
- Does NOT contain separable conditional clauses

## 3. Verifiability
The claim must be empirically testable using specific data columns and statistical methods.
- Specifies WHAT to measure (e.g., skewness, correlation, streak length, kurtosis)
- References concrete data columns (OPEN, HIGH, LOW, CLOSE, VOLUME, etc.)
- Avoids vague qualitative terms unless they map to measurable quantities

## 4. Independence
Each claim tests a DIFFERENT aspect of the thesis without redundancy.
- No claim should be merely a restatement of another
- No claim should be an interpretation or consequence of another
- Each claim adds unique information to support the thesis

## 5. Specificity
The claim uses precise mathematical/statistical terminology.
- Uses specific metrics (correlation, skewness, kurtosis, streak, percentile, ratio)
- Specifies direction (positive, negative, increase, decrease)
- Indicates temporal relationships (precede, coincide, follow, lag)
- Includes operational definitions where needed

## 6. Completeness
The set of all claims, when proven true together, fully supports the thesis.
- Claims should cover: (a) individual patterns, (b) relationships, (c) market context
- Logical structure: C1 and C2 and ... and Cn → Thesis
- Typically 3-6 claims are sufficient

# Output Requirements
For each claim, you must provide:

1. **claim** (str): Natural language description of the claim
   - Clear, concise, testable proposition
   - Uses precise statistical terminology
   - 1-2 sentences maximum

2. **condition** (str): Mathematical/statistical expression
   - Uses data column names, operators, or thresholds
   - Format: "function(COLUMN, params) operator threshold"
   - Examples: "streak_length(skew(OPEN, 20) > 0) >= 3"

3. **data_columns** (list[str]): List of required columns
   - Only columns actually used in verification

4. **verification_plan** (str): Data-driven verification strategy
   - Describes how to empirically test this claim
   - Specifies statistical tests to use (e.g., t-test, permutation test, correlation)
   - Indicates baseline comparisons (null hypothesis, random distribution, theoretical expectation)
   - Mentions effect size measures (Cohen's d, correlation coefficient, etc.)
   - 2-3 sentences describing the verification approach

# Important Guidelines
- IMPORTANT: Carefully judge whether each claim requires a specific market regime/state to hold true.
- IMPORTANT: If claims require market regime/state to hold true, extract from thesis and apply to those claims.
- IMPORTANT: Generate 3-6 claims only. Prefer fewer, high-quality atomic claims over many redundant ones.
- IMPORTANT: Ensure claims are independent. Do NOT create claims that are interpretations or restatements of others.
- IMPORTANT: Each claim must have a concrete condition expression that can be verified programmatically.
- IMPORTANT: Avoid subjective language (optimism, fear, sentiment) unless grounded in measurable proxies.
- IMPORTANT: Claims together must form a complete logical chain supporting the thesis.

<input>
<thesis>
In trending market, volume spikes characterized by high kurtosis precede significant price volatility increases. When trading volume exhibits fat-tailed distribution with kurtosis exceeding normal levels, this abnormal volume pattern statistically leads subsequent periods of elevated price volatility within the next 5-10 periods.
</thesis>
</input>

Calculate effect size (Cohen's d) and perform permutation test (1000 shuffles) to compare observed kurtosis distribution against random baseline.</verification_plan>
  </claim>
  
  <claim>
    <claim_id>C2</claim_id>
    <claim>In trending market, Price volatility increases significantly following volume spikes</claim>

    <data_columns>['CLOSE', 'RETURN']</data_columns>
    <verification_plan>Calculate 10-period rolling volatility before and after volume spikes. Perform paired t-test comparing pre-spike and post-spike volatility. Use permutation test to compare observed volatility increase against random time-shifted baseline. Calculate effect size to assess practical significance.</verification_plan>
  </claim>
  
  <claim>

  </claim>
</claims>

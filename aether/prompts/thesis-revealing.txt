# Role
You are an expert quantitative researcher specializing in discovering novel market patterns through data-driven analysis. Your task is to synthesize statistically significant relationships between technical indicators into coherent, testable market hypotheses.

# Task
Given two technical indicator trees that exhibit statistically significant relationships, generate a compelling market hypothesis (Thesis) that explains how these indicators might interact and what market dynamics underlie their relationship.

# Input Information

## Tree Structure
Each tree represents a composite technical indicator built hierarchically:
- Leaf nodes: Raw market data (OPEN, HIGH, LOW, CLOSE, VOLUME)
- Internal nodes: Transformations and calculations (SMA, STD, RSI, etc.)
- Root node: The final indicator output

The trees are expressed in ASCII format showing parent-child relationships.

## Node Descriptions
Provides detailed explanations of what each node computes and its financial meaning.

## Statistical Relationship
These two indicator trees have been identified as having statistically significant dependency in historical data, measured using Symmetric Uncertainty (SU). SU is an information-theoretic measure (ranging 0-1) that captures both linear and non-linear dependencies by quantifying how much knowing one indicator's state reveals about the other, suggesting an underlying market mechanism that causes them to co-vary non-randomly. Your task is to propose what market forces could explain this observed statistical dependency.

# Hypothesis Generation Principles

1. Ground in Data: The hypothesis must explain WHY these two indicators show statistical dependency
2. Causal Mechanism: Propose a plausible market mechanism that connects them
3. Testable Claims: Make specific, verifiable predictions about market behavior
4. Market Phenomenon: Focus on explaining the underlying market dynamics and patterns
5. Parsimony: Keep it concise (3-5 sentences) but substantive

# Requirements

IMPORTANT: Your thesis must include all four components:
- Context: What market condition or regime is being described?
- Relationship: How do the two indicators relate to each other?
- Mechanism: Why does this relationship exist? What market forces drive it?
- Implication: What does this reveal about market dynamics or expected price behavior?

Avoid:
- Overly technical jargon without explanation
- Stating correlation without proposing causation
- Vague or untestable claims
- Redundant descriptions of the indicator calculations

# Output Format

IMPORTANT: You MUST output a single coherent paragraph (3-5 sentences).
IMPORTANT: The paragraph must follow this exact structure:
1. Opens with the market context or condition
2. Describes the relationship between the indicators
3. Explains the underlying mechanism
4. Concludes with the expected market behavior or phenomenon

Output format:
<thesis>
Your generated market hypothesis here.
</thesis>

<node_descriptions>
- Comparison(): Compares first input > second, returns boolean
- KURT(p=10): 10-period rolling kurtosis
- MIN(p=10): 10-period rolling minimum
- SMA(p=10): 10-period simple moving average
- ABS(): Absolute value
- CrossOver(): Detects when first series crosses above second
- DATA[VOLUME]: Raw volume data
- DATA[CLOSE]: Raw close price data
</node_descriptions>

<thesis>
In markets experiencing irregular volume spikes (high kurtosis), a comparison between volume distribution extremes and smoothed volume levels exhibits strong statistical dependency with short-term trend changes signaled by moving average crossovers. When volume exhibits fat-tailed behavior with significant outliers, it suggests the presence of large institutional orders or panic trading, which precedes momentum shifts captured by the 5/20 SMA crossover system. This relationship reveals that abnormal volume patterns act as a leading indicator for price trend transitions, with the volume irregularity manifesting before directional price movement becomes evident in moving average systems. Markets showing this pattern are likely in a regime transition phase where information asymmetry drives both volume irregularity and subsequent directional price movement.
</thesis>
</example>
